Project Objectives & Challenges

Extreme Class Imbalance

Financial transaction logs are heavily biased towards legitimate activity, with fraud cases typically making up less than 1% of all logs (e.g. 0.17% in Credit Card and 0.13% in PaySim).

Asymmetric Cost Matrix

Missing a fraudulent transaction (False Negative) has major financial consequences ($COST_{FN} = 500$), whereas generating a false alarm (False Positive) is far cheaper ($COST_{FP} = 10$).

Our experiment framework targets a comprehensive evaluation of:

  • 5 Model Families: Logistic Regression, Decision Trees, Random Forests, Extra Trees, and XGBoost.
  • 6 Imbalance Handling Approaches: Baseline, Class Weighting, Random Undersampling, Random Oversampling, SMOTE, and ADASYN.
  • Validation-Based Threshold sweeps: Moving decision boundaries away from 0.5 to minimize the expected business cost.

Balancing Methods Impact

Resolving Fraud Class Recall

Standard models default to classifying all cases as legitimate because it yields 99.8% accuracy. Applying SMOTE, Oversampling, or Class Weighting forces the model to learn fraud features, increasing Recall up to 0.9866 (XGBoost SMOTE on PaySim).

The Precision Trade-off

Lowering decision thresholds to catch fraud increases false alarms. Under our cost ratio ($COST_{FN}=500$, $COST_{FP}=10$), this trade-off is highly profitable: incurring a few more false alarms (low precision) to avoid missed fraud is heavily favored.

Evaluation Metrics: ROC-AUC vs PR-AUC

In highly imbalanced contexts, ROC-AUC can show deceptively optimistic scores (e.g. 0.98+ for weak classifiers) because it relies on the False Positive Rate, which is diluted by the massive count of True Negatives:

FPR = FP / (TN + FP)

PR-AUC isolates the minority class performance directly, entirely ignoring the TN denominator:

Precision = TP / (TP + FP)

For example, PaySim Extra Trees with ADASYN achieves a high ROC-AUC of **0.9834** but its PR-AUC is a terrible **0.2849**, highlighting its operational failure.

Decision Threshold Tuning

Instead of assuming that 0.5 is optimal, our pipeline scans thresholds from 0.01 to 0.99 on validation data to minimize the expected cost.

SMOTE artificial minority sample generation shifts output probabilities upward. Calibration requires tuning validation threshold to a high 0.87, dropping test expected cost to 27,620.

Limitations & Future Work

Study Limitations

  • Concept Drift: Real-world fraud patterns shift dynamically; static evaluations degrade over time.
  • Scale Overhead: K-NN interpolation for SMOTE/ADASYN is computationally heavy on large sets.

Future Enhancements

  • LightGBM & CatBoost: Substantially faster training (5x-10x) and native categorical feature handling.
  • Graph Features: Graph embeddings (Node2Vec) mapping user/merchant relationships.

Operational Considerations

Latency Budget: Real-time financial transaction screening requires predictions in under 50-100ms. High-estimator ensembles must be compiled (e.g., via ONNX or Treelite) to run inline without transaction timeouts.

GDPR Compliance

Implementing SHAP explainability lets operational teams explain to end-users or regulators why a transaction was declined, satisfying European legal requirements.
