LABORATORY PROJECT No. 1
Financial Fraud Detection – Classification of Highly
Imbalanced Data
Project Objective
The objective of the project is to design, implement, and compare machine
learning models for detecting fraudulent transactions under conditions of
extreme class imbalance (e.g., 0.1–1% fraud cases).
Students are expected to:


understand the problem of class imbalance,



analyze the impact of different data balancing strategies,



compare classical and ensemble models,



evaluate models using appropriate metrics (not only accuracy),



conduct a cost analysis of incorrect decisions.

At least one dataset must be selected from Kaggle or the UCI Repository.
Example datasets:


Credit Card Fraud Detection Dataset (Kaggle) – 284,807 transactions,
~492 fraud cases,



IEEE-CIS Fraud Detection (more complex, requiring feature
engineering).

In real financial systems, the fraud class often constitutes less than 1% of the
data. A model may achieve 99% accuracy by classifying all observations as the
normal class.
At the same time, the cost of a False Negative error (undetected fraud) is usually
significantly higher than the cost of a False Positive.
Therefore, the project should focus on:


analysis of evaluation metrics under class imbalance conditions,



strategies for handling class imbalance,



cost-sensitive classification analysis.

1

Baseline models: Logistic Regression (baseline), Decision Tree, Random Forest,
XGBoost, LightGBM
Class imbalance approaches: No modification (baseline), Class weighting,
Random undersampling, Random oversampling, SMOTE, ADASYN, Threshold
moving.
Evaluation metrics – It must be demonstrated that: Accuracy ≠ an appropriate
metric in fraud detection.
Required metrics: ROC-AUC, PR-AUC (key metric), Recall (fraud), Precision,
F1-score, Confusion matrix, Expected cost (cost matrix)

Analyses required in the project:


The impact of balancing strategies on fraud Recall.



Decision threshold analysis (threshold tuning).



Model stability (k-fold cross validation). Comparison of models in terms
of effectiveness, training time, stability, and overfitting analysis.

Clarification of requirements for higher grades
From 4.0 and above, the report should include:


analysis of balancing methods’ impact on fraud Recall,



stability analysis (mean + standard deviation from CV).

From 4.5 and above:


decision threshold analysis,



overfitting analysis (train vs validation).

For 5.0:


cost analysis (Expected cost with cost matrix),



identification of study limitations (e.g., small number of fraud cases),



proposals for further system development.

Detailed scoring (40 pts)
1. Consolidated results table (all models + all metrics) – 6
2. Analysis of balancing methods’ impact on Recall (fraud) – 8
3. ROC-AUC and PR-AUC analysis (including plots) – 6
4. Stability analysis (k-fold: mean + standard deviation) – 6
5. Decision threshold analysis (threshold tuning) – 5
6. Cost analysis (Expected cost, cost matrix) – 5
7. Quality and maturity of experimental conclusions – 4
Total – 40 pts
